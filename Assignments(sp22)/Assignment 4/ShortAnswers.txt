A perfect matching is ideal in organ donor exchange networks (like a kidney donor chain), where matching every single patient with a compatible donor is the absolute priority. In this critical medical scenario, maximizing the total number of life-saving transplants made is far more important than optimizing for the absolute highest compatibility quality score of individual pairs. Leaving even one patient unmatched just to give someone else a slightly more optimal match could result in preventable loss of life.

In aviation safety, maximizing the synergistic quality and safety rating of each cockpit pair strictly outweighs the goal of keeping 100% of the active roster concurrently paired up.
